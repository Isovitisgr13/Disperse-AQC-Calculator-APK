    How it's calculated

    The bath is treated as perfectly mixed during each Aquachron step, with fresh water fed continuously. Feeding W litres through a bath of VTL litres lowers every dissolved substance by the same factor: C = C₀ · e^(−W / VTL), so W = VTL · ln(C₀ / C_end).

    On the reduction-clearing path, the dye dilution is split between the two wash-offs in the same proportion as the caustic dilution. Each phase uses whichever needs more water, dye or caustic. Caustic then ends at or below its targets, and the pH and acid dose use that actual value.

    If the flow needed is above the machine cap, the flow is held at the cap and the phase runs longer.

    pH uses the temperature-dependent water constant pKw(T). The acid dose comes from a charge balance with the weak acid's dissociation at the target pH. The alkalinity factor covers carbonate, silicate and fibre-bound alkali; calibrate it by titrating a bath sample.
